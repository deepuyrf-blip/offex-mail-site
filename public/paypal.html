Why PayPal asks for email verification

PayPal is a online payments platform. PayPal asks for your name, an email address and a password to open an account, and later for a bank or card to send money.

PayPal emails a confirmation link to verify the address; the account can receive money only after the email is confirmed.

Because the whole point of verification is to prove the inbox is real and reachable, any address that can receive mail works — including a throwaway one. A temp mail for PayPal gives you that reachable inbox without exposing the address you use for work, family or banking.

If the PayPal code doesn't arrive

If the PayPal code never lands, tap Resend once, then check whether you typed the address with a stray space — PayPal silently rejects malformed addresses.

  • Wait a full two minutes before requesting a new code — some senders queue mail during busy periods.
  • Re-check the address character by character; a single wrong letter sends the mail nowhere.
  • Refresh your Offex Mail inbox; new messages appear automatically, but a manual refresh never hurts.
  • Ask for the code once more, then generate a brand-new disposable address if PayPal keeps failing.
  • Confirm you are looking at the inbox that matches the address you actually typed into PayPal.

Common PayPal problems and fixes

  • Email not confirmed — open the confirmation link in the inbox, then re-check the address.
  • Account limited — PayPal limits new accounts that cannot be verified with a phone or bank.
  • Cannot recover later — financial accounts need a permanent inbox you control.

Is it safe to use a temp mail for PayPal?

A temporary inbox is a smart privacy tool: it keeps marketing mail, spam and data-broker lists away from your real address, and it disappears once you are done. For a one-time signup, a trial or a testing account, that is exactly what you want.

Two rules keep it safe: never use a temp mail for an account you need to keep, recover or pay for — PayPal included if you plan to rely on it — and always switch to a permanent address once a trial turns into something real.

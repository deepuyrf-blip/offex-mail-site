5 Practical Ways to Clean Background Noise From Voice Recordings

Audio Editing Guide · 5 min read

Background noise is one of the biggest reasons a recording sounds "amateur" even when the content is good. Fans, traffic, room echo and electrical hum all creep into audio recorded outside a professional studio. The good news is that most of it can be reduced significantly without expensive equipment. Here are five practical approaches, from prevention to post-processing.

1. Fix the Recording Environment First

No amount of software cleanup fully replaces a good recording environment. Soft surfaces — curtains, carpets, a closet full of clothes — absorb echo far better than a bare room. Recording a short test clip and listening back before the real session saves a lot of cleanup work later.

2. Get the Microphone Closer

Moving a microphone closer to the speaker increases the ratio of voice to background noise, which makes noise reduction software far more effective afterward. Even a shift from arm's length to about 15-20 cm away can make a noticeable difference.

3. Use Noise Reduction, Not Noise Removal

It's tempting to push a noise reduction setting to maximum, but over-processing introduces a "watery" or robotic artifact in the voice. A moderate setting that reduces noise by 70-80% usually sounds far more natural than a setting that tries to eliminate it completely.

4. Normalize After Cleaning, Not Before

Normalizing volume before removing noise can push the noise floor up along with the voice, making cleanup harder. Cleaning first and normalizing loudness afterward generally gives a better result.

5. Separate Voice From Background Music When Needed

If the noise problem is actually background music or another audio source layered under the voice — not room noise — standard noise reduction won't help much. That calls for a different kind of processing: voice and music (BGM) isolation, which separates the two into individual tracks so each can be handled on its own.

Quick Comparison

  • Room hiss / hum: noise reduction / enhancement processing works well.
  • Traffic or wind noise: a mix of recording position and moderate noise reduction.
  • Music playing under the voice: needs voice/BGM separation, not simple noise reduction.
